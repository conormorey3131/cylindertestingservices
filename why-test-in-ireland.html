Why Test Your Cylinders in Ireland

No ferry. No customs. No delay. Here's what shipping cylinders to Britain actually costs — and why testing them here doesn't.

1. Turnaround: days, not weeks

A cylinder sent to the UK for testing doesn't just travel there and back. It queues for freight, clears customs twice, waits its turn in a UK facility's schedule, and then makes the same journey home. Every one of those steps adds days — and a delay in any one of them adds more.

Testing at our Kilmallock facility removes the ferry crossing and the customs process entirely. Cylinders that would have been in transit for a fortnight can be tested, refurbished and back with you in days.

3. Keeping your EU (Pi) validity

VERIFY The paragraphs below are general guidance only. Exact wording must be checked and signed off with our notified body before this page is published.

Since the UK left the EU, periodic inspection carried out by a body appointed under Great Britain's own regulatory regime is recognised for use within Great Britain. It doesn't automatically carry EU (Pi-mark) validity.

This matters if your cylinders need to be placed on the market or used within the EU: to keep their EU Pi-mark validity, they typically need periodic inspection by a body notified under the EU framework — which is what we provide from Kilmallock.

We are the only facility in Ireland that is ISO 18119, TPED and ADR compliant and equipped to handle fire suppression system cylinders — so there's no longer any need to send that work across the Irish Sea.

This distinction concerns Great Britain specifically. It does not describe the position for Northern Ireland, which sits under different arrangements.

If you have cylinders that were last tested in Great Britain and you're not sure where that leaves you, see our FAQ: my cylinders were tested in the UK — are they still valid here?, or call us on (063) 20700 to talk it through.

4. Irish jobs, lower emissions

Every cylinder tested here is a cylinder that didn't need a ferry crossing and a return trip. That's a smaller transport footprint for your business, and it keeps skilled testing and refurbishment work — and the jobs that go with it — in Ireland rather than exporting it.
